## 4. The information may be wrong

Everything the Project shows you is best effort. It can be incomplete, out of
date, or simply incorrect. Known and expected sources of error include, without
limitation:

- **Stale requirement data.** Yale's catalog is large and changes every year.
  This Project is maintained by students in their spare time and will lag.
- **Transcript parsing errors.** Uploaded PDFs can be misread, including course
  codes, credit values, terms, and grades.
- **Policy interpretation.** Certificate rules, double-counting limits, and
  distributional tags reflect our reading of published policy. They are not
  official rulings and can differ from how Yale actually applies them.
- **Unmodeled rules.** Exceptions, petitions, substitutions, transfer and study
  abroad credit, departmental discretion, and individual accommodations are not
  fully modeled, and in many cases are not modeled at all.
- **Bugs.** The software may contain defects, may be unavailable, and may lose
  or corrupt data.

You are solely responsible for verifying every requirement, credit count, grade,
and conclusion before acting on it.
